A3 Design Card

**Overview**

Assignment 3 is different from the first two assignments because our program does not control forwarding. The harness moves customer packets; our job is to decide what the switches should observe, how they should summarize it, and what evidence should reach one small log. A separate detector then turns that evidence into a true-or-false answer.

Our design uses two related builds. The compact claim build records only the evidence needed for a specific detector and is designed to stay comfortably inside the smaller claim log. The truth build adds general packet samples, link-quality summaries, and drop information so the three truth attempts can reveal possible hidden eggs. Both builds use the same record format and reporting system.

**Customer activity**

We count source and destination bytes and packets directly in the switch data plane. This is more reliable than punting every packet to the controller because counters see every packet and do not consume the limited event budget. The truth build supports up to 63 shuffled customer ranges.

To avoid counting the same packet once at every hop, switches learn which ports connect to other switches from harness keepalives. Traffic is counted only when it enters from a customer-facing port. The claim build reports compact totals every half second. The broader truth build uses 1.5-second source windows and two-second destination windows so it can cover all 54 observed customers without filling the larger truth log. Packet counts and byte counts together reveal both the sending rate and the average packet size, which helps distinguish customers after their address ranges are shuffled.

This is how the long-pause detector identifies Spotify. It looks for a normally steady, full-sized, finite-rate stream and then checks for a gap of at least about ten seconds with traffic before and after it. It rejects small-packet customers, the much faster unlimited Steam flow, Amazon’s repeated eight-second off periods, and the two-second Spotify near miss.

**Links and failures**

Harness keepalives identify neighboring switches and provide sequence numbers and send times. Each switch records when a known neighbor has been silent for twenty keepalive periods and records a recovery if the neighbor speaks again. The two-second threshold avoided false failures during our high-load tests. The broken-link detector requires both directions of link 1-2 to end in the silent state. This rejects another failed link and a temporary 1-2 outage that later recovers.

The truth build also summarizes received keepalives, sequence gaps, average delay, and maximum delay every two seconds. This gives evidence for less obvious hidden problems such as a link that remains up but becomes slow or lossy.

**Traffic behavior**

Small data packets are punted only at the switch where they enter the network. The scanner detector groups them by source and asks whether one source contacted at least 50 distinct destinations within a five-second window. Counting distinct destinations rejects the “many packets to only a few addresses” near miss, while the time window rejects a scan spread across the whole run.

For truth runs, we retain small source/destination pairs so short scans remain visible. For each distinct ordinary flow signature, we keep its first two packets and then sample medium traffic at 1/1,024. Large traffic is sampled much more sparsely at 1/65,536, while counters still measure every packet. Our second truth run showed why this matters: sampling a very high-rate 1,000-byte Cloudflare flow used most of switch 7's controller budget and delayed reports from other switches. The new rule still identifies a newly planted large flow without letting a repeated hotspot crowd out link and counter evidence.

**Reporting and logging**

Switches batch observations into compact, length-prefixed binary records. The final truth build logs at switch 2, which is near the center but avoids the high-rate customer observed at switch 7. Routine reports take one decreasing-distance path to switch 2, vary equal-cost choices by report sequence, and avoid locally silent links. Failure evidence is repeated while a link stays silent, and both failure and recovery transitions are flooded over available links, because a changed link can also disrupt the normal report path. This keeps ordinary overhead low without making transition evidence depend on one route.

The final truth build was tested at 1.5x load on a replica of the published 15-switch grid across twelve scenarios, including a twenty-plant run and the exact 127/128/799/800-byte sampling boundaries. Healthy runs retained all 15 switches, all 44 directed link views, and all 836 expected link summaries without false failures. The twenty-plant run retained failures, recoveries, loss, delay, drops, pauses, three scans, and both medium and large side flows in 48,929 bytes. We also failed and recovered all 22 grid links individually; every case retained both directions and records from every switch. The largest 1.5x stress log used 51,398 of 65,536 bytes; a 4x hotspot test used 57,261 bytes. Compact mode still passes all three disclosed practice eggs at 10/10.

**Tradeoffs**

The main tradeoff is completeness versus budget. Counters are used for high-volume behavior, while punts are reserved for events where individual source and destination addresses matter. The claim build is conservative with bytes; the truth build spends more log space to discover unknown behavior.

The current detectors intentionally solve the three disclosed practice eggs, which earn no graded points. Hidden-egg detectors cannot be chosen correctly until the returned truth logs show what happened. After a hidden egg is identified, the intended process is to keep the same observation framework, remove unrelated records, and build one narrow detector that distinguishes the egg from its likely near misses.

All three practice detectors pass all ten of their positive, negative, and near-miss runs.
